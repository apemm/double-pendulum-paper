# Alt text

Written to AIP Publishing's alt-text guidance: one or two plain sentences of
about 25 to 50 words per figure or table, no file names, no panel-by-panel
narration, and no repetition of the caption. Numbering is that of the submitted
manuscript.

**Figure 1.** Line graphs of the logarithm of trajectory error against time for
six step sizes. The curves rise exponentially in parallel and level off at the
largest possible separation, and after division by the fourth power of the step
they collapse onto the growth curve of a perturbed initial condition.

**Figure 2.** Two scatter plots of the accurate-simulation horizon. In one it
rises linearly with the logarithm of one over the step, and in the other it
rises linearly with the number of significand bits, each with a fitted straight
line and a staircase predicted from the growth of a perturbation.

**Figure 3.** Line graph of the horizon against the logarithm of one over the
step for five floating-point precisions and for 237-bit arithmetic. The 237-bit
curve rises steadily, while each lower precision follows it and then levels off
at a plateau that is higher for more bits.

**Table I.** Four estimates of the largest Lyapunov exponent in natural units.
The long-time two-trajectory value is 0.299, and the three estimates that sample
only the first 160 time units, from a perturbed trajectory and from the horizons
against step and against precision, are 0.35.

**Table II.** For each of five floating-point precisions from 24 to 64
significand bits, the best step and the longest attainable horizon. The horizon
rises from about 53 natural time units at 24 bits to about 124 at 64 bits, which
is 17 to 40 seconds for rods one meter long.
