# Alt text for figures

AIP Publishing requires alternative text for every figure and table at
submission. Each entry describes what the figure shows to a reader who cannot see
it, and does not repeat the caption.

**Figure 1.** Three side-by-side diagrams of a double pendulum, each drawn as two
line segments hanging from a pivot at the origin. In every panel three pendulums
are overlaid: solid black, dashed orange, and dash-dotted green. In the first
panel, at time 39.5, the three lie exactly on top of one another. In the second,
at time 84.9, the orange pendulum points in a visibly different direction while
black and green still coincide. In the third, at time 102.9, all three point in
different directions.

**Figure 2.** Two stacked line graphs sharing a horizontal axis of time from 0 to
160. In the upper graph the vertical axis is the base-ten logarithm of the
separation, from minus 24 to 0. Six curves, for steps from two to the minus 6 down
to two to the minus 16, rise in parallel staircases from lower left to upper
right, each about two and a half units below the one before, and each flattens
just above zero at a dotted line marking the maximum separation. The curve for
the largest step flattens near time 75 and the curve for the smallest near time
150. In the lower graph the same curves have been divided by the fourth power of
the step and lie on top of one another along a single rising staircase, from
about 0 at time 25 to about 20 at time 155, each peeling off horizontally where it
saturates. A thin black line for a perturbed initial condition follows the same
staircase throughout.

**Figure 3.** A line graph of the base-ten logarithm of the separation, from
minus 32 to 0, against time from 0 to 240. Eight curves, for significand widths
from 16 to 96 bits, rise in parallel staircases at the same average slope. More
bits start lower and reach the top later: the 16-bit curve saturates before time
25, the 53-bit curve near time 100, and the 96-bit curve near time 220.

**Figure 4.** Two scatter plots with fitted straight lines. In the left plot the
horizon rises from 43 at k equals 4 to 148 at k equals 17, where k is the base-two
logarithm of one over the step. The points scatter about an orange fitted line by
up to about ten time units, and a gray staircase curve passes through every point.
In the right plot the horizon rises with the number of significand bits from near
0 at 11 bits to about 240 at 128 bits, for three step sizes shown as squares,
circles and triangles that lie close together along one orange fitted line, with
a gray staircase through the circles.

**Figure 5.** A line graph of horizon against k, the base-two logarithm of one
over the step, from 4 to 17. A black line for 237-bit arithmetic rises steadily
from 43 to 148. Five colored curves with markers, for 24, 32, 40, 53 and 64 bits,
follow the black line at small k and then leave it one after another and become
roughly horizontal: the 24-bit curve near 50, the 32-bit curve near 57, the
40-bit curve near 74, the 53-bit curve near 97, and the 64-bit curve near 115. The
24-bit curve declines to 30 at the right-hand edge.

**Figure 6.** A line graph with time from 0 to 160 on the horizontal axis and the
base-ten logarithm of an error, from minus 17 to 1, on the vertical axis. A black
staircase for the trajectory error rises from minus 12 at time 10 to 0 at about
time 100 and stays there. An orange curve for the energy error fluctuates between
about minus 15 and minus 11 for the whole run and never rises above minus 10.

**Table I.** Four estimates of the largest Lyapunov exponent: 0.299 from the
two-trajectory method over twenty thousand time units, 0.33 from the growth of a
perturbation, 0.35 plus or minus 0.02 from horizon against step, and 0.35 plus or
minus 0.01 from horizon against precision.

**Table II.** For significand widths of 24, 32, 40, 53 and 64 bits, the best step
is two to the minus 7, 7, 9, 11 and 15, and the longest horizon is 53, 66, 79, 101
and 124 natural time units, or 17, 21, 25, 32 and 40 seconds for rods one meter
long.
