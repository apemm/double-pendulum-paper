# Statement of appropriateness

AJP's submission form asks for a brief statement of why the manuscript is
appropriate for publication under the Statement of Editorial Policy. Paste this.

The manuscript describes a computational exercise for upper-division courses in
computational physics, classical mechanics, or numerical analysis, and it is
written for instructors rather than for researchers. It presents no new physics.
The results it demonstrates, that the time for which a simulation of a chaotic
system is accurate grows only logarithmically with the accuracy of the
integrator and the precision of the arithmetic, exist in the research literature
for the Lorenz system, and the manuscript cites that literature. What it adds is
a classroom treatment on the double pendulum in which students separate
truncation error from roundoff error, measure each, and obtain the Lyapunov
exponent from the numerical error itself. The exercise needs about forty lines
of code, runs in minutes on a laptop, and ends with four suggested problems. The
code, data, and two animations are supplied as supplementary material.
